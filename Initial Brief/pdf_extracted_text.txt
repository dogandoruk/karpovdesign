Goals
karpovdesign.com is the portfolio and front door for Karpov Design LLC, a North Carolina
residential design and consulting practice. It should win residential clients, sell
downloadable designs, and teach. The owner must be able to add projects himself by
dropping files into a folder, with no coding.
The site must:
1. Show every project on one scrolling page as a hero image plus a short info card,
filterable by category.
2. Open a full gallery page for each project, with a way back to all projects.
3. Take client inquiries, and explain the workflow, services and fees.
4. Sell downloads (plan sets, templates, CAD files) in a webshop.
5. Carry news that mirrors Instagram and other social posts.
6. Host an education area with YouTube videos and a Q&A forum.
7. Update itself from a shared folder, using simple file-naming rules (see Updating the
site).
Who visits: homeowners and small developers looking for a residential designer; builders
and contractors; people buying plan sets; students and designers following the education
content.
Look and feel: clean, image-first, lots of white (or dark) space, project images doing the
talking, minimal text on the main pages. It should work equally well on a phone.
Wording rule (legal): the owner is not yet a licensed architect. Nowhere on the site may
the words "architect", "architecture" or "architectural" describe the company or its
services. Use "residential design", "design consulting" and "Karpov Design" instead. Old
project text from karpovad.com must be reworded where it uses these terms. Past
employment at architecture firms and the degree names can stay as facts.

Design mindset and the home page
The site should sell how the owner thinks, not only finished pictures. High-end clients hire
a person and a process, so the home page leads with sketching, making and the path from
idea to building.
Home page, top to bottom
1. Full-screen opening reel. A silent, looping montage of about 30 seconds: 3–5 second
clips of hand sketching and watercolour, a physical model being made, a 3D model
turning, a render resolving, a site visit, a finished room. Clips alternate with still images.
Over it, one short line (for example "Homes designed around how you live") and two
buttons: See projects and Start a project.
A visible pause button (accessibility).
On phones and slow connections, show a still image or a lighter reel so the page
loads fast.
Clips and stills come from a folder 6 Home reel , played in file-number order ( 01
Sketch.mp4 , 02 Model.jpg …), so the owner can refresh the reel himself.
2. How I design. A drawn process graphic: five or six stages, each shown with images
from one real project as it develops (first sketch → massing study → plans → 3D view →
construction → finished photo). One line per stage on what the client decides and
receives. It links to the full Workflow page.
3. Selected work. Three featured projects, large.
4. Why work with me. Three or four short value points backed by facts, such as
competition wins, built work across four countries, BIM and construction-document
experience, and seeing the design in 3D before anything is built.
5. Client words, latest news, and a final "Start a project" call to action.
Logo animation
The KAD mark is built from lines, and they can animate: the lines slide in from the screen
edges and lock into the square mark, then the mark settles into the header as the opening
reel fades up. It's a nice signature if it stays brief, and it shouldn't be a gate.
No "click to enter" splash page. An extra click before any content loses visitors and
hides the site from search engines. The animation plays over the opening reel, which is
already loading behind it.

All-projects page
Layout: one project per row (or a two-column grid on wide screens). Each block = the
hero image, large, plus the info card beside or below it.
Info card fields: Title, Year, Category, Type, Program, Size, Location, Client, Role, Status.
Team appears only on the project page, since some team lists are long.
Default order: newest first, with an optional "Featured" flag that pins a project to the
top.
Category filter bar at the top, sticky while scrolling: All · Single-Family Residential ·
Multifamily Residential · Commercial · Cultural · Academic · Conceptual · Urban
Planning · Furniture · Art. Clicking one rearranges the list with a short animation; no
page reload.
A project can carry more than one category (a student housing concept is both
Multifamily and Academic and Conceptual), so it shows under each.
Optional second filter: Status (Built · In progress · Competition · Concept), since
clients often want to see built work only.
Images load lazily as you scroll, so a page of 30+ projects stays fast.
Project page (one per project)
Opens when the hero image or title is clicked. Its address is readable, e.g.
karpovdesign.com/projects/outside-in.
Top: hero image full width, then the title and the full info card, including Team.
Middle: the project description (2 to 4 short paragraphs).
Gallery: every image for the project in the order given by the file numbers. Clicking an
image opens a full-screen viewer with arrows, swipe on phones, and Esc to close.
Optional captions from the file name.
Optional extras: a PDF (presentation board or brochure) download; a short video or 3D
walk-through link.
Navigation: a "← All projects" button at top and bottom that returns to the list with the
same filter still selected and the same scroll position. Also "Previous / Next project"
links.

=== PAGE 11 ===
Other sections
Five more sections round out the site. Each is listed with what it must do; wording and
prices come from the owner.
Inquiry portal (Contact)
A guided inquiry form, not just a blank message box. Fields: name, email, phone, project
location (city, state), project type (new home, addition, renovation, multifamily,
commercial, consulting, other), approximate size, budget range, desired start date, how
they heard about us, message, and file upload for a survey, lot plan or photos.
On submit: an email to the owner with all answers, an automatic "we received your
inquiry" email to the client, and a copy saved in a simple list (a spreadsheet or the site's
admin).
Spam protection that doesn't make people solve puzzles (an invisible check).
Optional: "Book a 30-minute intro call" with a calendar booking link. The owner works
from Turkey, so bookings should show times in the visitor's own time zone.
Contact details on the page: email, US phone number, service area statement (North
Carolina and where else the owner works), social links.
Services, workflow and fees
Services: short cards, each with an icon or image, one paragraph and a typical
deliverable. Starting list: residential design (new homes), additions and renovations,
multifamily design, design consulting, drafting and construction drawings, 3D modeling
and visualization, permit drawing sets.
Workflow: a numbered, drawn step diagram of how a project runs, for example: inquiry
→ intro call → proposal and agreement → site and needs survey → concept design →
design development → construction drawings → permit support → construction
questions. Each step has one or two lines on what the client gets and what they decide.
Fees: explain how pricing works rather than list fixed prices. Show the fee methods
used (for example fixed fee per phase, per-square-foot ranges, hourly rate for
consulting), what affects the price, and what's included or extra (site visits, revisions,
engineering by others). End with a call to action to the inquiry form. Figures come from
the owner.
A short FAQ on this page: Do you work outside North Carolina? Do I need an architect
or engineer too? How long does a typical house design take? How do revisions work?
How do remote meetings work?

Client consulting (design consultations)
The site offers two kinds of paid one-to-one session, and they must never be mixed: client
consulting is for people planning a building, and student consulting is for people learning
design and software. Each has its own page, its own button wording, its own booking type
and its own prices, so a homeowner never lands on a Revit lesson and a student never
books a design consultation.
The booking tool holds the two as separate session types with separate intake forms.
Inquiries from each go to separate labels in the owner's inbox.
A paid one-to-one video session for anyone with an idea to talk through: a homeowner
weighing a lot or a renovation, a builder testing a layout, a developer sizing a project.
The owner sketches live, reviews plans or photos, and sends a short summary
afterward.

Webshop (downloads, books, courses)

How it works
1. The owner keeps one cloud folder, Karpov Design Website, synced to his computer
(Google Drive or Dropbox, designer's choice).
2. To add a project, he copies the blank template folder, renames it, drops in images, and
fills in info.txt .
3. The site checks the folder automatically (for example every hour, plus a manual
"publish now" button or link).
4. It resizes and compresses the images, builds the project page, adds the card to the
Projects list under the right categories, and publishes.
5. The owner gets an email: what was published, plus any problems in plain language
("2026 Smith Residence: the Year line is empty" or "Category 'Single Family' not
recognised, did you mean Single-Family Residential?").
6. A folder that breaks the rules is skipped, never half-published. The rest of the site
keeps working.
